# Brand voice

## The brand name

**KidTechies** is the brand. "AI for kids" is a descriptor that may sit beneath
it. Never treat "AI for Kids" as a second brand name in a title, footer, byline
or signature - two brands split every search, share and word-of-mouth the
company earns (dossier S02). Pending client confirmation, decision D3.

## Who we are talking to

A parent. Always a parent, or a teacher. **Never a child.** Even when the
product is for children, the reader is the adult holding the card.

## How KidTechies sounds

- **Warm, plain, never patronising.** The parent is an intelligent adult who
  happens not to know this subject. That is not a deficiency and we never imply
  it is.
- **Explains uncertainty rather than hiding it.** The product teaches children
  that AI guesses and gets things wrong. Copy that pretends to certainty
  contradicts the curriculum.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Your child watches an AI mistake a muffin for a
  chihuahua, then works out why" beats "develops critical AI literacy skills."
- **Short sentences carry the weight.** Long ones are fine when they earn it.
- **Never uses fear.** Not fear of the child falling behind, not fear of
  technology, not fear of other parents doing more. This is a hard rule and it
  lives in `child-safety-rules.md` as well as here.

## Three paragraphs we would be proud of

> Your child's school just started teaching AI. If you are not sure what that
> means, you are in good company - most of us learned about computers from
> children who were better at them than we were. This is a way to catch up
> together, in about eight hours, at whatever pace suits a Tuesday evening.

*Why: meets the Vision 2030 parent's real question in the first line. Admits the
gap without shaming anyone. Specific about time. No urgency, no pitch.*

> AI does not know things. It guesses, very well, from what it has seen before.
> That is the whole idea, and a nine-year-old can hold it easily once they watch
> a machine confidently call a muffin a chihuahua. After that, the news about AI
> reads differently - to them and usually to you.

*Why: teaches something true in four sentences. The image does the work. The
last clause flatters the parent without flattering them falsely.*

> Your child never gets a login or an email address. The account is yours; your
> child is a nickname and an age group — no surname, no date of birth, nothing
> else. And the first mission needs no account at all. We built it that way
> because we could not think of a good reason to do otherwise.

*Why: the strongest differentiator, stated flatly and truthfully. Specific about
what is stored, which is more credible than claiming nothing is. The last
sentence undercuts the boast, which is what makes it land.*

*Replaced 2026-10-07. The previous example opened "Nothing about your child
leaves the device… Progress lives in the browser" — true of guest mode, false for
parent accounts, whose children's progress is stored server-side. It was a
template agents imitate, so a false example here propagates into every draft.*

## Three paragraphs we would reject

> Unlock your child's FULL POTENTIAL in the AI revolution! Don't let them get
> left behind - the future belongs to those who start TODAY.

*Why rejected: fear of falling behind, manufactured urgency, capitals, an
outcome promise we cannot evidence. Every one of these is a hard fail.*

> Our cutting-edge, gamified AI literacy platform leverages an immersive
> narrative framework to deliver scaffolded computational thinking outcomes
> aligned to 21st-century competency standards.

*Why rejected: written for a grant panel, not a parent. Says nothing a person
could repeat at dinner. "Outcomes aligned to standards" is a claim we have no
evidence for.*

> Hey superstar! Ready to become an AI Detective? Grab a grown-up and let us go
> on an adventure!!

*Why rejected: addressed to the child. Marketing copy speaks to parents and
teachers only - this is an advertising-standards matter in the Gulf, the EU and
the US, not a stylistic preference.*

## Wording patterns

| Prefer | Over |
|---|---|
| "understand AI" | "master AI" / "become an AI expert" |
| "8-9 hours of guided learning" | "23:48 of animated film" |
| "buy once, keep it" | "lifetime access" (implies an account) |
| "try the first mission" | "start your free trial" (implies signup) |
| "your child" | "your little one", "kiddo", "superstar" |
| "we are still building X" | silence, or a vague "coming soon" |
| "about eight hours" | "8.5 hours" (false precision) |
