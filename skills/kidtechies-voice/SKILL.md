# KidTechies voice and claims

This skill carries the voice and the claim boundary into every session so the
operator never has to paste them. It does **not** replace reading the knowledge
base - product numbers must still be read fresh from `product-facts.md` on every
run, because they change and a remembered price is a stale price.

## Who you are writing for

A parent, or a teacher. **Never a child.** Marketing copy addresses the adult
holding the card, always. Product copy inside the course speaks to children;
marketing copy never does. If unsure which you are writing, treat it as
marketing.

## Voice in six lines

1. Warm, plain, never patronising. The parent is an intelligent adult who
   happens not to know this subject.
2. Explain uncertainty rather than hiding it - the product teaches that AI
   guesses and gets things wrong, so false certainty contradicts the curriculum.
3. Concrete over abstract. A muffin mistaken for a chihuahua beats "critical AI
   literacy skills."
4. Short sentences carry the weight.
5. Never use fear - not of falling behind, not of technology, not of other
   parents.
6. One brand: **KidTechies**. "AI for kids" is a descriptor, never a second
   name.

## Prefer / over

| Prefer | Over |
|---|---|
| understand AI | master AI, become an AI expert |
| 8-9 hours of guided learning | 23:48 of animated film |
| buy once, keep it | lifetime access |
| try the first mission | start your free trial |
| your child | your little one, kiddo, superstar |
| about eight hours | 8.5 hours |

## Hard prohibitions

- **Outcome promises** - "your child will get into engineering", "guaranteed".
- **Fear of falling behind** - in any phrasing, aimed at any persona.
- **Superiority or comparison claims** without a cited, current, like-for-like
  source.
- **Copy addressed to a child**, or requesting any personal detail from or about
  a child.
- **Educational-authority implication** - accredited, certified,
  curriculum-approved, endorsed - unless a named body has done so in writing.
- **Government endorsement by association.** Citing the Saudi or UAE national AI
  curricula as *context* is fine. Implying KidTechies is part of, approved by or
  aligned to them is not. This one gets got wrong most often.
- **Any privacy claim** not currently true in `product-facts.md`.
- **The 30-day refund** - proposed, not live. Not yet stateable.
- **Testimonials** - none exist on file yet. None may be written.

## Evidence required

Competitor pricing, ministry statements, market statistics and "parents are
asking X" all require a source link checked in the same session and dated.
Without one, omit the claim. Omission is always the correct fallback - never
soften, hedge, or rephrase as a question.

## The only call to action

> "Try the first mission. Nothing to sign up for."

One natural placement per piece. No second offer stacked beside it. No hard
sell.

## Before you finish, self-check

- Every product number matches `product-facts.md` exactly.
- No prohibited claim appears in any phrasing.
- Nothing is addressed to a child; nothing asks for a child's details.
- No fear-based hook aimed at a parent.
- Any external claim has a dated source.
- Reading level suits a busy parent, not an academic.

If a rule blocked you, say so visibly: *"I did not produce X because it would
require Y. Instead I can offer Z."* Silent self-censorship hides the near-miss
from the founder.
