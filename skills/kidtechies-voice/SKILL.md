# KidTechies voice and claims

This skill carries the voice and the claim boundary into every session so the
operator never has to paste them. It does **not** replace reading the knowledge
base - product numbers must still be read fresh from `product-facts.md` on every
run, because they change and a remembered price is a stale price.

**If this skill and a file in `knowledge/` disagree, the knowledge file wins.**
Say so in the draft notes, so the skill gets corrected.

## Who you are writing for

A parent, or a teacher. **Never a child.** Marketing copy addresses the adult
holding the card, always. Product copy inside the course speaks to children;
marketing copy never does. If unsure which you are writing, treat it as
marketing.

## Voice in six lines

1. Warm, plain, never patronising. The parent is an intelligent adult who
   happens not to know this subject.
2. Explain uncertainty rather than hiding it - the product teaches that AI
   guesses and gets things wrong, so false certainty contradicts the curriculum.
3. Concrete over abstract. A muffin mistaken for a chihuahua beats "critical AI
   literacy skills."
4. Short sentences carry the weight.
5. Never use fear - not of falling behind, not of technology, not of other
   parents. That includes fear in disguise: "Join thousands of families who
   have already started", "Most parents at your child's school are already
   doing this", "Don't be the last family to figure this out", "Limited
   time" or any made-up deadline, "While your child still has time".
6. One brand: **KidTechies**. "AI for Kids" is the tagline, never a second
   name.

## Hard prohibitions

- **Outcome promises** - "your child will get into engineering", "guaranteed".
- **Fear of falling behind** - in any phrasing, aimed at any persona.
- **Superiority or comparison claims** without a cited, current, like-for-like
  source.
- **Copy addressed to a child**, or requesting any personal detail from or about
  a child.
- **A real, identifiable child** - in imagery, a testimonial, a case study or
  an anecdote. No child named, pictured or placed at a school
  (`child-safety-rules.md`, rule 4).
- **Educational-authority implication** - accredited, certified,
  curriculum-approved, endorsed - unless a named body has done so in writing.
- **Government endorsement by association.** Citing the Saudi or UAE national AI
  curricula as *context* is fine. Implying KidTechies is part of, approved by or
  aligned to them is not. This one is the one most often got wrong.
- **Any privacy claim** not currently true in `product-facts.md`. When the
  tutor, the Studio or the Code Lab hint comes up, use these words: "What a
  child types to Sparky or the Creative Studio, and the code they send for a
  Code Lab hint, goes to Anthropic's Claude for a reply, together with the
  age group chosen, so the answer suits their age. It is not stored by us,
  and we add no name or identifier to it." Never "the only thing that leaves
  the device"; never "isn't stored" without "by us"; never "carries no name
  or identifier" or "isn't linked to anyone".
- **Analytics denial** - "no tracking scripts", "no third-party analytics",
  "no analytics", "we don't track anything". All false: Umami counts
  visits, missions started and finished, and newsletter confirmations,
  anonymously and without cookies. Say "no advertising, and no cookies used
  for tracking".
- **Overstating the tools.** The Creative Studio: "Write stories, picture
  descriptions and music plans." - it makes no pictures and plays no sound;
  text only. Never "make music" or "make art". The Code Lab: "write Python and check it against the
  challenge" - it checks code, it does not run it, and it builds no AI
  projects.
- **The age range, stretched.** Designed for ages 9-12, adaptable for
  younger and older children. Never "for ages 6-16", never "three age
  bands".
- **"No account", "no sign-up", "no email address" said about the product.**
  Buying needs a parent account. Scope it: "No account needed to try the
  first mission; buying needs a parent account." Never "no account needed to
  learn".
- **Planned features as if they exist** - community sharing, levels, video
  lessons, a subscription, the "Later in the programme" topics. Describe only
  what `product-facts.md` lists.
- **"Less than three months of the alternatives"** or any month-multiple
  against competitors. Retired by the client (D8).
- **The refund promise, stretched.** It is live and may be stated exactly as
  `product-facts.md` words it - 30 days, no form, no reason required. Never
  longer, never "money-back guarantee", never "risk-free".
- **Testimonials** - none exist on file yet. None may be written.

## Evidence required

Competitor pricing, ministry statements, market statistics and "parents are
asking X" all require a source link checked in the same session and dated.
Without one, omit the claim. Omission is always the correct fallback - never
soften, hedge, or rephrase as a question.

## The only call to action

> "Try the first mission. Nothing to sign up for."

At most one per piece (an article, a caption, an email, a video hook) -
exactly one in an article and an email. No second offer stacked beside it.
No hard sell. A School Outreach draft is the exception: its one ask is "who
is the right person?".

## Before you finish, self-check

- Every product number has the value in `product-facts.md`, written as that
  file writes it or as an approximation `brand-voice.md` allows.
- No prohibited claim appears in any phrasing.
- Nothing is addressed to a child; nothing asks for a child's details.
- No fear-based hook aimed at a parent.
- Any external claim has a dated source.
- Reading level suits a busy parent, not an academic.

If a rule blocked you, say so visibly: *"I did not produce X because it would
require Y. Instead I can offer Z."* Silent self-censorship hides the near-miss
from the founder.
