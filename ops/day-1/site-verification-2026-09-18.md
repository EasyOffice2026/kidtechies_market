# Site verification — 18 September 2026

`product-facts.md` before walking into the client room.

### Four things the dossier got wrong

1. **The refund promise exists.** The dossier says the site has no visible
   refund promise; we had it marked PROPOSED and forbidden. It is real, well
   written, and buried on the terms page. Promoted to a usable fact — the site
   job is to *move* it to `/pricing`, not to write one.

2. **Age structure is not three bands.** Site: *"ages 9–12, adaptable for 6–8
   and 13–16."* The dossier presents `6–8 · 9–12 · 13–16` as equal tracks. These
   describe different products, and the difference is the kind a parent notices
   after paying. Copy rule corrected.

3. **The pricing page already carries the hours claim.** It shows "23:48 of
   animated film" *and* "around 8–9 hours of guided learning." The dossier's
   Blocker 02 reads as though the hours framing is absent. The fix is which one
   leads — a smaller job than briefed.

4. **A $100 anchor price**, struck through, "Save $30 · 30% off." Not mentioned
   anywhere in the dossier. It is a discount argument sitting where the dossier
   wants a permanent-value argument, and the two undercut each other. Client
   decision added to the fact form.

### Still unverifiable

28 activities, 70 quiz questions, printable certificate, printable sheets, the
characters Maya/Pip/Glitch, and the exact free-tier boundary. All remain PENDING
and unusable by agents.

## Two things to raise in the session

**The printables.** The teacher and homeschool multiplier — 15% of effort, and
the persona that reaches thirty families at a time — is sold almost entirely on
printable sheets solving a classroom where half the children have no device. We
could not find any evidence they exist. If they do not, Agent 6's outreach
premise needs rethinking before the first batch goes out, and queue item 14
should not be written.

**The thin fourth course.** Missions are distributed 5 / 4 / 3 / 2. "Smart and
Safe AI Heroes" is the smallest at two missions, and the dossier names it as the
course the worried screen-time parent — roughly a quarter of the market — buys
*first*. The entry point for that persona is the least built-out thing on the
site. Not a marketing problem to solve, but the founder should know it before
marketing drives traffic at it.

## Two things worth noticing

The privacy wording on `/for-parents` is genuinely good — plain, absolute, no
self-congratulation. Agents should reuse it verbatim rather than paraphrase.

The terms page tells readers to contact *"whoever operates this installation of
AI for Kids."* That is unmodified template text on the page a parent reads
before paying and a school's data-protection officer reads before replying. It
is the cheapest trust fix on the site.

## Caveat

The sitemap's `lastmod` is `2026-09-03T17:52:02.596Z` on every URL — the same
date as the Growth Dossier. Either the site has not changed since the dossier
was written, or the timestamps are generated and tell us nothing. Do not read
these findings as "the site is now correct"; read them as "this is what was
published on 18 September."
