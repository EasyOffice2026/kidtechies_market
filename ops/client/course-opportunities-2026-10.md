## What the evidence says this month

**Children are using generative AI faster than anyone is teaching it.** UNICEF
found children adopting AI more than three times faster than adults, in a
survey of about 1,000 children aged 12–17 in each of ten countries. A third of
those children worried about AI being used to scam people or spread
misinformation; a quarter feared their images being turned into explicit
deepfakes. ([UNICEF, 30 June 2026](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/children-are-adopting-ai-technologies-more-three-times-faster-adults))

**Most teenagers use it; a quarter just copy what it says.** In the UK, 79% of
13–18 year olds used generative AI in 2026. More of them now check what it
tells them (52%, up from 43%) — but 24% said they simply copy its output.
([National Literacy Trust, August 2026, 40,543 young people](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/young-people-teachers-and-parents-use-of-ai-to-support-literacy-in-2026/))

**Schools now teach seven areas, and KidTechies covers about three.** The UAE's
national AI subject, taught from kindergarten to Grade 12, covers: foundational
concepts, data and algorithms, software tools, ethical awareness, real-world
applications, innovation and project design, and policy and community issues.
Middle school goes into bias in algorithms and ethics; secondary school into
writing instructions for AI. ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/education/uae-one-of-worlds-first-countries-to-teach-ai-in-schools) ·
[Education Middle East](https://educationmiddleeast.com/news/policy-and-regulations/uae-public-schools-to-teach-ai-from-kg-to-grade-12/))

**Competitors teach children to make things with AI, not to see through it.**
CodaKid runs Generative AI and Prompt Engineering courses for ages 8 and up
built around making memes, videos and code with AI tools.
([CodaKid](https://codakid.com/ai-classes-for-kids/))

## Recommendations, ranked

### 1 · Extend "Smart and Safe AI Heroes": Real or Made? — do first

**Two or three new missions** on how AI makes pictures, voices and text, and how
to tell when something was made rather than captured.

- **The strongest evidence of the three.** It answers the fear children report
  most — scams, misinformation, deepfakes — directly.
- **It fixes the thinnest course.** Smart and Safe has two missions while the
  others have three to five, and it is the course a safety-minded parent buys
  first.
- **It is KidTechies' own idea, applied again.** The product's promise is
  "understand how AI gets things wrong". Competitors teach children to make
  deepfakes; this teaches them to catch one.
- **Cheapest to build.** It extends an existing course with the same
  characters, style and price, so it needs no new course page or product.

Ages: the core 9–12, with the usual notes for 6–8 and 13–16.

### 2 · New course: Ask, Then Check — next

A reframed version of the "Prompt Engineering" course already on the coming-soon
list: how to ask an AI clearly, and then **how to check what it says** before
using it.

- The UAE teaches writing instructions for AI in secondary school, and the UK
  figures show a quarter of teenagers copying AI output unchecked. The checking
  half is the gap.
- It suits the 13–16 band best, which is the age band KidTechies serves least
  today.
- Positioning matters: "get AI to do your homework" is what parents fear.
  "Ask well, then check" is what they want, and it is consistent with every
  other KidTechies course.

### 3 · Hold for more evidence

- **AI in Science.** It fits the curriculum's "real-world applications", but
  this month found no demand signal from parents. Agent 1 keeps watching.
- **Web Dev with AI.** We'd suggest reconsidering this one. It moves
  KidTechies into coding-school territory, where CodaKid and Tynker are strong
  and cheap, and away from the position that makes KidTechies different:
  understanding AI rather than programming it.
